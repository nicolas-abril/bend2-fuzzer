# Triage of the 2026-09-04 eight-hour run

Differential run of bend2-core at `15b53ff` (Emit Nat matches as tables
or one flat chain) with `../bend2-fuzzer/fuzz.ts`, driven in 63
ten-minute chunks with fresh base seeds, lanes rotated three
sequential-only chunks to one each with the CPU-parallel lane
(`--threads 4`), the Metal lane (`--metal`) and both. Chunk time 7.5 h
(451 min), wall time eight hours with the triage in between.

| lanes | chunks | seeds | ok | skips | findings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| interp + C-seq + JS | 32 | 192,000 | 191,864 | 31 | 105 |
| + threads 4 | 10 | 66,000 | 65,957 | 10 | 33 |
| + Metal | 10 | 2,700 | 2,699 | 0 | 1 |
| + threads 4 + Metal | 11 | 2,380 | 2,379 | 1 | 0 |
| total | 63 | 263,080 | 262,899 | 42 | 139 |

Neither extra lane produced a lane-specific finding: every failure
reproduces on the sequential C leg (the one Metal-chunk finding and the
33 thread-chunk findings are all class B or A crashes at emit or in
C-seq), and every thread-lane skip was load. The 139 findings collapse
into eight root causes, A–I (G was folded into E); every one is
pre-existing, reproducing on 69be65f, the commit before the Nat chain
work. Six are solved since and listed in the summary; the two still open
are detailed below. Skips: 26 worker timeouts (12 load, 9
unary-volume trees, plus the five load cases in the thread lane), one
8-member `cc-timeout` batch (class F) and 9 counted `trans-ulp`
disagreements, each analysed in the Skips section.

## Findings by root cause

### D. A long flat U32 chain nests past clang's 256-bracket wall — cc-fail, 2 findings

Finders: seed 1823680294889734456 (chunk 39, sequential legs) and seed
8251088115608358593 (chunk 49; compiles and prints its JS values with
the aliasing patch): `fatal
error: bracket nesting level exceeded maximum of 256` in the
continuation segment of the result def, whose seal-combine fold is a
left-nested chain of 78 U32 operations. Repro:
`repros/c_bracket_wall_chain.bend`, a def whose `+r` let is one
expression of 80 alternating `+`/`.^.` steps on its parameter;
threshold probed at 64 steps compiling and 68 failing (each `U32_BIN`
level expands to about three brackets, so ~76 source levels reach 256).
JS is unaffected (bun has no such limit). Root cause: `emit_intr`
substitutes each argument's expression text straight into the
intrinsic's template (`tpl_run(it.C, ws)`), so a chain of intrinsic
applications becomes one nested C expression; nothing ever spills an
intermediate to a local. Pre-existing (the same shape was the bend3
`bracket_wall` class; that line's chunking never existed in
bend2-core's emitter).

Verified fix (scratch worktree; chains of 80 and 130 then compile and
print the JS values, the seed prints its JS output):

```
-  const out = tpl_run(it.C!, dup ? ws.map((a) => emit_alias(fl, a, "a")) : ws);
+  const out = tpl_run(it.C!, ws.map((a) => dup || a.length > 600
+    ? emit_alias(fl, a, "a") : a));
```

i.e. hold an argument in an `a_N` local once its text is long; the
cut-off of 600 characters keeps every benchmark's expressions inline
(a 64-step chain is about 1.5 kB, a typical intrinsic argument under
100 bytes) — re-verify byte-identity on the benches before adopting a
threshold. Status: OPEN, patch verified; 2 in 290,000 seeds, the
generator rarely builds chains this long.

### F. A big literal is emitted as straight-line allocations and clang goes superlinear — cc-timeout (8 skips, one batch)

Chunk 51 skipped one whole batch of eight members as `cc-timeout`
(seeds 13224377051126233928, 1823662231803035443, 14887718926613571505,
6178347796739880797, 3487004107290373020, 17579062616063079282,
7841689672227218374, 10533033361676726151; one timestamp). The first
member alone reproduces it: its source holds a literal
`List<(Char & String)>` of 1326 pairs (one 22,972-character line, the
generator's `big` feature), and the emitted C builds it inline: 1326
`Tuple` and `Con` nodes, 1326 `Chr` words and 7610 `SCon` cells, 10,262
`heap_alloc`/`term_ctr` pairs in ONE segment (`FID_XT1000057_K41`,
30,861 of the file's 36,452 lines). Timings on this machine:

| step | time |
|---|---|
| emit (`--to`) | 6.7 s |
| `clang -std=c11 -O1` (the fuzzer's flags) | 186 s |
| CLI `-o` (Metal attempt then `-O3`) | 337 s |

The fuzzer's C compile cap is 120 s, so the batch was skipped rather
than failed; the program is otherwise fine (JS runs it at once). Not a
correctness bug but a real compile-time cliff: clang's time on a
30,000-line basic block of allocations is far from linear, and a
constant structure this size wants to be emitted as data (a CONSTV
table walked at startup, or a builder loop) rather than as code.
Status: FIXED on nicolas3 (2026-09-07, a8917dc (efead59 before the rebase onto e913e65)):
a constant constructor literal is laid out once in a static region of
the corpus (`STAT_IMG`, copied in at boot) and referenced as one term,
never built. The witness (1326 pairs, 20000 rebuilds) emits in 1.1 s
instead of 6.7 s, compiles with `cc -O3` in 0.3 s instead of 27.9 s and
runs in 85 ms instead of 254 ms; lexer and symreg speed up 16-18%, the
other benches emit the same bodies.

### worker-timeout, seed 7326092177038764404 (chunk 3) — benign, load

The sealed spelling's interpreter worker passed 45 s while the raw one
finished. In isolation raw takes 9.0 s and sealed 9.3 s. The cost is
`tl7000003`, thirty rounds of `U32.read(U32.show(a0))` in a Nat loop,
run through base's structural Word code (the known ~1000x interpreter
gap for Word chains); the rest (four `bt(5n)` fork trees, two more Nat
loops of 13 and 41 steps) is cheap. The cap was crossed because the
chunk shared the machine with the delta-reducer runs of chunk 2's
findings. No compiler or performance issue.

### worker-timeout, seed 10819503677034647564 (chunk 9) — benign, unary Nat volume

In isolation raw takes 37.8 s and sealed 37.0 s, just under the cap, so
any concurrent load crosses it. Timed piecewise: `mk4000011` builds a
BINARY tree of depth `(1197664583 % 65536) % 10 = 9`, 1023 nodes, and
each node holds a Nat literal (`331n` on the branches, `771n` at the
leaves) that `rd4000004` converts with `U32.from_nat`; one walk of the
tree is 7.6 s, and the program walks it three times (`us4000001` does
`rd(s) + rd(s)`, then `rd4000004(sh)` once more) for 22.9 s of the
total. A single `U32.from_nat(597n)` is 22 ms, so this is the known
~1000x interpreter cost of base's structural Nat/Word code multiplied
by a thousand conversions, not a cliff: the compiled legs run it in
milliseconds. No compiler or performance issue.

### trans-ulp, 9 counted skips (chunks 22, 31, 37, 61, 62) — backend libm divergence, quantified

These are counted, not saved with seeds, so they were characterized
directly: 199,221 random finite floats through every `f32_*`
transcendental in OPERATIONS, C as `f32_rewrap(kf(f32_unbox(x)))`
(macOS single-precision libm, clang -O1) against bun's
`Math.fround(Math.k(x))`. Bit-exact mismatches, all one ULP:

| function | mismatches | share |
|---|---|---|
| atan | 80812 | 40.6% |
| atan2 | 40687 | 20.4% |
| tan | 7652 | 3.8% |
| tanh | 209 | 0.10% |
| exp | 84 | 0.04% |
| pow | 51 | 0.03% |
| sinh, cosh, asin, acos, cos, sin | 23–36 each | ~0.015% |
| log | 3 | |
| sqrt, log2, log10, fmod | 0 | |

Example: `atanf(-8.5e22)` is `-1.5707962513` (0x3FC90FDA) in libm and
`-1.5707963705` (0x3FC90FDB, the float nearest pi/2) from bun. JS computes
in double and rounds once, so it is correctly rounded almost always;
macOS's `atanf`/`atan2f`/`tanf` sit within their 1-ULP allowance but
not on the correctly rounded value near saturation. This is the
"each backend's libm" divergence the fuzzer already downgrades to a
skip, so the verdict stands; but NOTES.md's line that bun's Math
"agrees bit-exactly with macOS libm floats on everything probed" is
wrong for these three functions and should be amended. Not a compiler
issue.
